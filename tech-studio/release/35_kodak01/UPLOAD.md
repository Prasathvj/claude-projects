**Title:** Kodak invented the digital camera… then buried it #shorts

**Description:**
In December 1975, Kodak engineer Steve Sasson built the world's first portable, self-contained digital camera. It was the size of a toaster, weighed about 3.6 kg (8 lb), ran on 16 AA batteries and captured 0.01-megapixel (100 × 100 pixel) black-and-white images. Saving one photo to a cassette tape took 23 seconds. Kodak's management reportedly reacted with "that's cute — but don't tell anyone about it" (as quoted in The New York Times, 2008), and the prototype was never put into production. Kodak's money came from film: in 1976 it sold about 90% of all photographic film in the US, and every photo meant film, developing and prints. Digital photos need none of that. In 1988 Kodak had over 145,000 employees; on 19 January 2012 it filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, and its digital-imaging patents were later sold for about $525 million. The company that invented digital photography was disrupted by it.
