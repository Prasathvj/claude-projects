In 45 minutes,
a company lost 460 million dollars.
No hacker.
Just one missed step.
August 1st,
2012. Knight Capital,
one of America's biggest stock traders,
is updating its trading software.
Eight servers.
The new code reaches only seven.
The eighth still has an old
feature, switched off since 2003:
Power Peg.
And the new code reuses its
old switch.
8:01 AM.
The system sends 97 warning emails.
Nobody acts.
9:30. The market opens.
Power Peg wakes up.
Orders fly out nonstop:
four million trades in 154 stocks.
45 minutes later,
they finally stop it.
Over 460 million dollars, gone.
Within a week,
Knight needs a 400 million dollar
rescue. Within a year,
a rival takes it over.
The lesson?
Delete dead code,
and never deploy alone.
