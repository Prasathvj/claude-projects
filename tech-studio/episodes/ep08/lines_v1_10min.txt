Its big strengths are using a computer, writing code, research and science, and long professional tasks.

Here's who gets it. On the Plus plan, OpenAI includes GPT-6 Astra inside two places: ChatGPT Work and Codex, not the regular chat list.
On the Pro, Business and Enterprise plans, you also get GPT-6 Pro, a version that runs on Astra for the toughest questions.
And on the Free and Go plans, Astra isn't included. You'll still get OpenAI's everyday models, which are very capable for normal chats.
Even on Plus, there are limits. OpenAI estimates roughly five to forty-five Astra messages every five hours, depending on how heavy each task is.
So treat Astra like a precious resource. Use it for big jobs, not for "write me a birthday message".
Before anything, check which plan you're on in your ChatGPT settings. Free, Go, Plus or Pro. That decides everything.
Now, how do you actually use it? Step one: install the ChatGPT desktop app, on Windows or Mac, and update it to the latest version.
Step two: at the top, switch from Chat to Work. That's where Astra lives. Then pick Astra from the model menu.
Step three, the exciting part: Computer Use. Open Plugins, find Computer Use, install it, and turn on its switches.
On a Mac, you'll be asked to allow Screen Recording and Accessibility. On Windows, keep the app you want it to use visible on screen.
Step four: start your request with "at Computer" for desktop apps, or "at Chrome" for jobs inside your browser.
Then describe the task clearly. Which app, which file, and what "done" looks like.
For example: "Open my sales spreadsheet, make a chart of monthly totals, and save it as a PDF on my desktop."
Before it touches any app, ChatGPT asks for permission. You can allow it once, or always allow apps you trust.
And you can watch every click. If something looks wrong, you stop it.

Now, "GPT-6 Astra free" is one of the biggest searches right now. Be careful.
Searches for it in India have exploded, and scammers follow the searches.
Websites and APK files promising free, unlimited Astra are a classic way to steal accounts and card details.
The safe places are chatgpt.com, the official ChatGPT apps, and OpenAI's own API. If a deal sounds too good, it is.
And here's the warning OpenAI gave itself. Astra is the first OpenAI model rated "Critical" for cybersecurity under its own safety framework.
In OpenAI's framework, that level means a model could find serious security holes, or plan an attack, with very little human help.
So OpenAI added extra safeguards. Monitors watch Astra's tasks and can pause or stop them mid-run, and OpenAI admits this can sometimes interrupt legitimate work.
OpenAI also said Astra is harder to monitor than its previous model. That's a big reason it watches it so closely.
For you, the practical lesson is simple. Only give Astra access to the apps and files a task really needs.
Here are five tips before you start.
One. Give Astra the full picture. The goal, the files, and what a finished job looks like.
Two. Break huge jobs into stages, and check its work after each stage.
Three. Never let it near banking apps, passwords or payment pages.
Four. Use Sol for small things, so your Astra limit lasts for the big ones.
Five. Double-check facts and numbers. Even the smartest model can be confidently wrong.
