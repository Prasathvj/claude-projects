**Title:** MH370 vanished. A satellite kept hearing it for 7 hours. #shorts

**Description:**
At 1:21 a.m. on 8 March 2014, Malaysia Airlines flight MH370 (Kuala Lumpur to Beijing, 239 people on board) vanished from air traffic control. The last words from the cockpit were "Good night, Malaysian three seven zero"; moments later the transponder went dark. Military radar saw the plane turn back across Malaysia and fly out over the Andaman Sea, until it slipped beyond radar range at 2:22 a.m. But an Inmarsat satellite kept exchanging automatic "handshakes" with it: seven in total, the last at 8:19 a.m. Each carried only a timing value and a frequency shift. From those signals alone, investigators traced the flight to the "7th arc" in the southern Indian Ocean. In July 2015 a flaperon (part of the wing) washed up on Réunion Island and was confirmed to be from MH370. Searches have scanned well over 100,000 km² of seabed; the latest (Ocean Infinity, 2025–26) ended in 2026 with nothing found. The 2018 Malaysian safety report concluded the turn-back was most likely flown manually, but who turned the plane around, and why, is still unknown.

We remember the 239 passengers and crew of MH370 and their families.
