## Short 1: Quicksand
Facts (searched 2026-11-17; secondary sources citing Nature 2005, Bonn et al.; NPS Glen Canyon alert):
- A person cannot be pulled completely under: the body floats, sinking only to about waist depth (Nature study, via National Geographic/LiveScience).
- Movement liquefies the sand; struggling makes you sink deeper. Pulling a foot out of saltwater quicksand can need a force comparable to lifting a car.
- Advice: stay calm, spread your weight (lean back), remove a heavy backpack, slowly wiggle the legs to let water into the gaps.
- Real dangers: exhaustion, cold, and a rising tide in tidal areas (a 2023 Alaska mud flats death, news report). NPS: "rarely life-threatening, but escaping can be tiring."
- Avoid: ground that is wet, loose or unusually smooth; hike with a buddy; test the ground.
- Not claimed: exact escape times, that struggling always sinks you "to the neck", the Khaldoun name.
Lines (one picture each):
1. Stuck in quicksand. Do you struggle, or stay calm? Pick one.  [V1 stuck, startled]
2. Movies say it swallows you whole. That is a lie.  [V2 movie-style sinking gag]
3. Your body floats, usually only to your waist.  [V3 stuck to waist, calm]
4. The real problem is panic. Struggling stirs the sand and you sink deeper.  [V4 flailing, splashing]
5. So lean back, spread your weight, and slowly wiggle your legs.  [V5 leaning back, wiggling]
6. The real killers are exhaustion, and a rising tide.  [V6 tide rising behind him]

## Short 2: Lightning and the tree
Facts (NWS, CDC, NOAA, NPS pages found 2026-11-17):
- No place outside is safe when a thunderstorm is in the area. If you hear thunder you are likely within striking distance; lightning can strike up to 10 miles from a storm.
- Being under a tree is the second leading cause of lightning casualties (NWS). Never shelter under an isolated tree (CDC).
- Safe options: a substantial building, or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows closed. Not a convertible or open vehicle.
- Stay inside until 30 minutes after the last thunder (NWS).
- Victims carry no charge and are safe to touch; call 911, start CPR if not breathing.
- NOT claimed: the "lightning crouch" (not found in sources), strike statistics.
Lines:
1. Thunderstorm, and you are outside. Do you hide under a tree? Pick one.  [V1 stormy field, tree]
2. Movies say a tree is shelter. It is not.  [V2 hiding under tree, flash]
3. Standing under a tree is the second leading cause of lightning casualties.  [V3 flash and tree, shock]
4. Hear thunder? You are close enough to be struck. It can reach ten miles from the storm.  [V4 sky map/radar or wide storm]
5. Get inside a sturdy building, or a hard-topped car with the windows up.  [V5 hero safely in car]
6. And stay in for thirty minutes after the last thunder.  [V6 hero at window, clock]
7. Comment your answer. Follow for the next movie lie.  [V7 outro, Bit]
Title: Never hide under a tree in a storm (Movies Lied To You)
Sources: NWS lightning safety overview; CDC lightning safety; NOAA JetStream lightning safety; NPS lightning safety.

## Short 3: Shark (DRAFT, awaiting approval). Searched 2026-11-21.
Facts:
- Florida Museum (ISAF): if a shark bites, claw at its eyes and gill openings, two sensitive areas. Hits to the snout may give only temporary relief; an aggressive shark often returns and each later hit to the snout is less effective, so use any chance to escape.
- Experts disagree on the snout: some sources say a hit may make the shark pull back briefly, others say punching underwater is weak and not your best defence. Script says "not so fast" and does not claim the nose never works.
- Stay calm, keep the shark in view, leave the water slowly (several sources). Playing dead does not work (several sources).
- ISAF 2025: 65 unprovoked bites worldwide, in line with the 2020 to 2024 average of 61 a year. Bites are rare. Fatality counts differ between sources, so none in the script.
- NOT claimed: "sharks smell a drop of blood from miles away" (sources conflict), exact odds, species claims.
Lines:
1. A shark is coming straight at you. Do you punch it on the nose? Pick one.
2. Movies say a punch on the nose saves you. Experts say, not so fast.
3. Hits to the snout may only help for a moment, and the shark can come back.
4. If it bites, go for the eyes and the gills.
5. Stay calm, keep it in sight, and get out of the water.
6. Playing dead does not work. And bites are rare: about sixty a year, worldwide.
7. Comment your answer. Follow for the next movie lie.

Sources: Florida Museum ISAF (swimmers and divers safety pages; 2025 report); National Geographic shark attack tips; Smithsonian Magazine; NPR "shark attacks what to do".

## Short 5: Bear (DRAFT, awaiting approval). Searched 2026-11-21.
Facts:
- You cannot outrun a bear: grizzlies about 35 to 40 mph, black bears about 30 mph; a top human sprinter about 27 mph (Outdoor Life, Field & Stream). Speeds vary by source, script says "faster than any human".
- Bears climb trees, both black and grizzly (PopSci), so a tree is not an escape.
- Several sources: do not run or scream; speak calmly and back away slowly (MeatEater, secondhand; NPS page itself not confirmed in results, CHECK nps.gov/subjects/bears/safety.htm before publishing).
- Black bear attack (NPS): do not play dead; get to a secure place like a car or building; if not possible, fight back with anything you have.
- Grizzly/brown bear: guidance differs by species and attack type (secondhand: play dead if a grizzly attacks defensively). Script does NOT give a species-specific rule; it says to check the park's bear page.
- Bear spray: carry EPA-approved spray; use only on a charging or aggressive bear (NPS).
- NOT claimed: attack statistics, exact distances, a universal "play dead" rule.
Lines:
1. A bear is walking toward you. Do you run? Pick one.
2. Movies say run for it. That is a mistake.
3. A bear can run faster than any human, and it can climb trees too.
4. Stay calm. Speak in a low voice, and back away slowly.
5. Never play dead with a black bear. Get to a car or building, or fight back.
6. Hiking in bear country? Carry bear spray, and know how to use it.
