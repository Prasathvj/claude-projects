## Fact-check notes (checked 2026-11-17)

| Claim in the script | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Drop to hands and knees, cover head and neck with one arm and hand, hold on until shaking stops | [Ready.gov: earthquakes](https://www.ready.gov/earthquakes) |
| No sturdy table: crawl next to an interior wall, away from windows | Ready.gov: earthquakes |
| In bed: stay there, cover head and neck with a pillow | [FEMA / Ready.gov information sheet](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2021-12/ready_earthquake-information-sheet.pdf) |
| Driving: pull over to a clear area away from buildings, trees, overpasses, underpasses and utility wires | FEMA / Ready.gov information sheet |
| Stay in the car | Ready.gov STEP guide (see the note below) |
| Outdoors: stay outside, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines | Ready.gov: earthquakes |
| The doorway idea comes from collapsed adobe homes; in modern buildings doorways are no safer and do not protect from falling objects | [ShakeOut: Drop, Cover, Hold On](https://www.shakeout.org/dropcoverholdon/) |
| Running outside is especially dangerous: glass, bricks and building pieces can fall | ShakeOut |
| "Triangle of Life" does not apply to buildings in the United States; drop, cover, hold on is still best | [USGS FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-triangle-life-and-it-legitimate) |
| Expect aftershocks; drop, cover, hold on again | Ready.gov: earthquakes |
| Check yourself for injuries; for a medical emergency call; otherwise text, do not call | Ready.gov: earthquakes |
| Smell gas: leave the building and call from outside | [Red Cross: earthquake safety](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/earthquake.html); [USGS: after an earthquake](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-do-i-do-after-earthquake); Ready.gov (do not enter a damaged home if you smell gas) |

Notes to check yourself:
- **Driving.** FEMA says stop in a clear area. The Ready.gov STEP guide says stay in the car. The script combines them
  ("pull over to a clear area ... then stay in the car"). They agree in practice, but they are different documents.
- **The USGS Triangle of Life line** is specifically about buildings in the United States. The script says so.

Left out on purpose, because I could not confirm them in an official source:
- "Do not light a match or flip a switch" after a gas smell (widely repeated, not confirmed here).
- Any number of seconds for the shaking, and any earthquake kit list.
- Any advice for tall buildings, elevators or being near the coast.

## Long video

Puppet hero and dog on your living room (L2) plus illustrated scenes for the myths. Tip cards between beats.

| # | Narration (Fenrir) | Acting direction | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The ground starts shaking. The shelves rattle. What do you do right now? | Bright, urgent hook | Scene S1: the room shakes, hero and dog startled |
| 2 | Most people run for a doorway, or run outside. Both are mistakes. | Firm, slightly dramatic | Living room (puppets), screen shake |
| 3 | Here is what experts say to do instead. Drop. Cover. And hold on. | Clear, steady | Living room (puppets), hero points |
| 4 | Drop to your hands and knees. Cover your head and neck with one arm. And hold on to something sturdy until the shaking stops. | Firm, teacher voice | CARD: DROP, COVER, HOLD ON |
| 5 | Get under a sturdy table or desk if there is one. If not, crawl next to an interior wall, away from windows. | Practical | Scene S2: hero and dog under the coffee table |
| 6 | Myth one: stand in a doorway. That idea comes from old adobe houses. In modern buildings, a doorway is no safer, and it does not protect you from falling objects. | Serious, myth-busting | Scene S3: hero in a doorway, red X |
| 7 | Myth two: run outside. Glass, bricks and pieces of the building can fall on you. Stay put, and get under a table. | Urgent | Scene S4: hero running out, debris falling, red X |
| 8 | Myth three: the triangle of life. The USGS says it does not apply to buildings built in the United States. Drop, cover and hold on is still the best advice. | Matter-of-fact | CARD: THREE MYTHS |
| 9 | In bed? Stay there. Cover your head and neck with a pillow. | Calm | Scene S5: hero in bed, pillow over head |
| 10 | Driving? Pull over to a clear area, away from buildings, trees, overpasses and power lines. Then stay in the car. | Clear | Scene S6: car pulled over in the open |
| 11 | Outside? Stay outside. Move to an open area, away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines. | Clear | Scene S7: hero crouched in an open field |
| 12 | When the shaking stops, expect aftershocks. If one comes, drop, cover and hold on again. | Serious | Living room (puppets), hero drops |
| 13 | Check yourself for injuries. For a medical emergency, call emergency services. For everything else, text instead of calling, so the phone lines stay free. | Practical | Living room (puppets), sticker "TEXT, DON'T CALL" |
| 14 | Smell gas? Leave the building, and call from outside. | Short, firm | Scene S8: hero and dog leaving the house |
| 15 | Drop, cover, hold on. Stay put. Text, do not call. | Quick recap | CARD: THE RECAP |
| 16 | Follow for the next scenario. Your move. | Cheeky, upbeat | Living room (puppets), hero waves |
