# Your Car Is Stuck in Snow. What Do You Do? (Winter Survival #1) DRAFT, awaiting approval. Searched 2026-11-21.
Facts:
- Stay with the vehicle unless safety is no more than about 100 yards away (CDC; Michigan State Police/NHTSA). Walking in a storm is dangerous.
- Keep the exhaust pipe clear of snow before running the engine; blocked exhaust lets deadly carbon monoxide into the car (Mass.gov, Michigan).
- Run the engine only sparingly, about 5 to 10 minutes an hour for heat, not continuously (NWS-based advice via Fox Weather, Michigan). Crack a window a little for fresh air (NWS-based, secondhand; one local agency warns CO can build up even with a clear pipe).
- Call 911 if you can (Mass.gov). Use hazard lights.
- Getting unstuck (AAA): clear snow from around all wheels and under the car, do not spin the wheels, move a little at a time, back up then drive forward over compacted snow; rocking too long can damage the transmission; call roadside help if still stuck.
- Stay awake; take turns sleeping if with others (Michigan State Police).
- NOT claimed: floor mats or cat litter tricks (unconfirmed); exact times beyond "5 to 10 minutes an hour"; survival-time numbers.
- CHECK before publishing: the CDC "Stay safe in winter" page and weather.gov "Winter storms" page for the engine-and-window wording.
